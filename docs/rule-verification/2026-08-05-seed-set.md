# Seed set verification — 2026-08-05

**Status: research, not sign-off.** Every rule in the pack is still
`status: "draft"` after this pass, and that is deliberate. Promotion to
`active` means *a person read the primary source*, and this document was
produced by an agent reading those sources. It exists so that the person doing
the sign-off checks a reading rather than doing twelve from scratch.

Read it with a copy of each statute open. Where a claim below is wrong, the
rule is wrong.

## Findings that need a decision

### 1. ~~Oregon is wrong by up to 30 days~~ — FIXED (NEH-400)

**Resolved 2026-08-05, after this document was first written.** Kept rather
than deleted: a finding and its fix are more useful together than a fix alone,
and the reason it survived a full fixture suite is the part worth remembering.

All three Oregon rules used `anchor: "formation-month", dayOfMonth: "last"` —
the end of the anniversary month. The statutes say the anniversary **date**:

> ORS 65.787(1): "A domestic corporation, and a foreign corporation authorized
> to transact business in this state, shall **by the corporation's anniversary**
> deliver to the Secretary of State for filing an annual report…"

ORS 60.787(1) and ORS 63.787(1) use the same phrase for business corporations
and LLCs. The Oregon SOS agrees: *"Your renewal is due on the anniversary date
of the original filing."*

So a company formed on 3 March was told 31 March. **Late, not early** — the
direction that costs someone a penalty.

**The fix** is a new cadence anchor, `formation-anniversary`, which takes the
month *and day* from the entity. Additive to `rule.v1.json`, so every existing
v1 rule is untouched. Leap days need no special case: 29 February clamps to the
28th in a common year, which is exactly what ORS 65.001 defines the anniversary
to be.

**Why it survived a full fixture suite, which is the real lesson.** The only
Oregon fixture was formed on **31 January** — a month end, where the two
cadences produce the same date. Every Oregon assertion passed while the rule
was wrong for every entity not formed on a month end. A leap-day fixture would
not have caught it either, because February's month end *is* where a leap-day
anniversary clamps to.

Only a **mid-month** formation can tell the two apart. `OR_NONPROFIT_MID_MONTH`
is formed on the 14th precisely because the 14th agrees with nothing — not a
month end, not a leap day, not the first.

Generalised: **a fixture whose value coincides under both the right and the
wrong implementation tests nothing, and reads exactly like a fixture that
does.** When adding a boundary case, ask which wrong implementation it would
catch. If the answer is none, it is documentation.

### 2. The Washington charities rule is two filings wearing one hat

The rule triggers on *soliciting* **or** *holding $250,000+ in charitable
assets*. Those are two different registrations with two different agencies-
within-an-agency, two forms, two fees, and two deadlines:

| | Charitable **solicitation** registration | Charitable **trust** registration |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | RCW 19.09 | RCW 11.110 |
| Trigger | soliciting contributions | assets *"invested for income-producing purposes, exceeding a value of two hundred fifty thousand dollars"* (WAC 434-120-305) |
| Renewal | last business day of the 11th month after the accounting year (WAC 434-120-140(2)(a)) | **not established by anything read here** |
| Fee | $40 (RCW 19.09.062(2)) | not established |

The $250,000 figure appears **nowhere in RCW 19.09**. Chapter 19.09's only
threshold is the *opposite* kind — RCW 19.09.081 **exempts** an organisation
raising under $50,000 whose work is entirely unpaid, which this rule does not
express either.

Two further details: the WAC says *"exceeding"*, so `gte` is off by one at
exactly $250,000; and an endowed charity currently gets told to file the
*solicitation renewal*, which is the wrong form at the wrong price.

**Not split here**, because WAC 434-120-310 does not state the trust
registration's deadline, and inventing one is the failure this project exists
to avoid. The false negative that NEH-228 fixed is real and its regression test
stays — but the obligation belongs in a rule of its own.

### 3. No Washington statute sets the annual report due date

> RCW 23.95.255(4): "Annual reports must be delivered to the secretary of state
> **on a date determined by the secretary of state**…"

So the end-of-formation-month cadence in all three WA annual report rules rests
on the SOS's administrative practice, not on any statute. The requirement is
statutory and verified; the *date* is not, and its source needs a citation of
its own — probably a WAC section, the way the charities deadline turned out to
live in WAC 434-120-140.

### 4. Two Washington fees could not be read from the agency

`sos.wa.gov` returns **403 to all automated retrieval**, including its own PDF
forms. Search results attributed to that domain say:

- profit corporations and LLCs: **$70**, not the $60 in the pack — *probably a
  fourth error*
- RCW 24.03A nonprofits: $60, **reduced to $20** where gross revenue was under
  $500,000 — a conditional fee the schema cannot express at all

Neither was changed. A search summary is not a primary source, and this project's
own rule is that copying a secondary source imports its errors. **Someone has to
open that page in a browser.**

### 5. The schema cannot roll a deadline backwards

WAC 434-120-140(2)(a) says the charities renewal is due the **last business
day** of the eleventh month. `dayOfMonth: "last"` gives the last *calendar* day,
so when a month ends on a weekend the rule shows a date up to two days **late**.

`weekendRule` offers only `"roll-forward"`. This needs the opposite.

### 6. Delaware's $50 is not the number a customer will pay

8 Del. C. § 502 sets the 1 March deadline and no fee; the $50 report fee's
source was not located. What *was* located is § 503, and it reframes the whole
entry:

> the tax shall be "more than $200,000 nor less than $175" (authorised shares)
> or "more than $200,000 nor less than $400" (assumed par value)

So the minimum real cost is at least 3.5× the fee shown, and the maximum is
4000×. Showing `$50` as the cost of a Delaware annual report is not a rounding
issue — it is a different order of magnitude, and the rule must not go `active`
until the UI surfaces the note or the schema can express a computed fee.

Two modelling choices inside those, both deliberate and neither an IRS rule:

- **990-EZ's `> $50,000` lower bound** is this pack's invention. The IRS lets a
  small organisation *elect* 990-EZ instead of 990-N; the bound exists so the
  two are mutually exclusive and a small charity is not told it owes both.
- **990-N's threshold is "normally" ≤ $50,000**, an averaging test across
  years. The pack evaluates a single year, so an organisation briefly over the
  line may still qualify.

## Method, and what it cannot tell you

Sources read directly: `app.leg.wa.gov` (RCW and WAC),
`oregonlegislature.gov` / `oregon.public.law`, `delcode.delaware.gov`,
`irs.gov`, and the Oregon SOS fee schedule PDF. Every quoted passage above was
read from one of those.

Not read: `sos.wa.gov`, which blocks automated retrieval entirely (§4).

**No compliance vendor, blog, or law-firm summary was used for any value in the
pack.** Where only a search summary existed, the value was left unchanged and
flagged rather than updated — which is why finding #4 is a flag and not a fifth
row in the corrections table.

**What this pass structurally cannot establish**, and what the human sign-off is
for:

- whether a statute has been amended since the source page was last published
- whether the agency's *practice* differs from the statute, which is exactly
  where Washington's due dates live
- whether an obligation exists that no rule in the pack covers at all — reading
  twelve rules against twelve sources finds errors in what is there, never the
  gaps

That last one is the real limit. This pass says the pack has four fewer errors
than it did. It does not say the pack is complete, and M1 wants 15–20 rules
against the 12 here.
